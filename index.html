Active inference, by running it

Walking in the dark

The power goes out at night, in a flat you have lived in for years.

You do not stand still. You do not wait for a torch. You get up and you walk, and somewhere along the way you put a hand out and touch a wall, and you carry on walking, and you arrive at the door.

Something happened on that walk that is worth stopping on, and it is not the arriving.

You put your hand out expecting a bookshelf, and what you touch is a table. You are surprised, and the surprise is the useful part, because it can only happen if you were expecting something in the first place. You were not moving blind. You were carrying an expectation, and the table has just contradicted it.

Two things follow, and they are worth keeping apart. The first is that you now think differently about where you are. You are further along than you thought, or you drifted left in the dark, and that revision is important to plan the next step. The second is optional, and depends on what you came for. If you were only passing through, you carry on to the door with a corrected sense of your position. If you wanted the bookshelf, you now go and look for it, and where you look is decided by the sense of your position you have just corrected.

Notice what did not happen. You did not freeze, and you did not go back to where you started because you got confused and had to begin again.

You were not holding the belief that the bookshelf was in front of you as a unique truth. You thought it more probable than anything else, but you never discarded the other options. That is the proposal active inference can make: not a claim about where you are with absolute certainty, but a standing account of how plausible every place is, revised as things arrive.

The hand that finds a table does not end the walk, it changes what you think is likely. What you were most sure of loses ground, what you had almost dismissed gains it, and everything you had worked out before the mistake is still there. Recovery is not a repair you carry out afterwards. It is what happens by itself when you were never committed to one answer in the first place.

This tutorial is about writing this mechanism as numbers you can run, and about how those numbers move when evidence arrives.

Notice how little the table actually told you. Not where you are, not to a floorboard. Past the bookshelf, along that wall, somewhere in a stretch of about two metres. You did not refine it further and nothing in the walk asked you to. The exact answer, the one that would say precisely where you stand, was never available to you in the dark and would have cost more than it was worth. You went to the door on something rough, and rough was enough.

You also kept moving while you were unsure, and the more you moved, the less certain you were about where you stood. Any of those steps could have been slightly long or slightly short, and you had nothing to check them against. Nothing failed. You lost confidence in your own position simply by acting without a point of reference, and confidence lost that way does not come back on its own.

Then there was the moment you stopped. You put a hand out deliberately, not to get anywhere, but to find out where you were. You gave up a step to buy an answer.

In a flat you did not know, you would have made that trade far more often. You would have touched things you had no need of, opened your hand against surfaces that told you nothing about the route, simply because you had never touched them before.

And through all of it you were going somewhere. Not accumulating points, not maximising anything you could name. Going to the door.

Seven questions, one walk. Each of the seven is a module here, in that order, and each one ends with code small enough that you can read every number in it.

What this is not

It is not a library, and not a benchmark. For real work, pymdp is the reference implementation. The purpose is comprehension, not deployment.

The modules

  1. Module 1. Where am I? An agent that does not know where it stands keeps a belief over every cell, updated with Bayes' rule.
  2. Module 2. Moving Moving blurs the belief, looking sharpens it again.
  3. Module 3. Free energy Free energy scores an approximate belief when the exact one is too costly to compute.
  4. Module 4. Expected free energy The agent chooses actions by scoring which future both informs it and suits it.
  5. Module 5. Novelty and an uncertain model The agent learns its own model by counting, and novelty drives it to where counts are few.
  6. Preferences, and what they cost Preference as a distribution, and four limitations shown rather than claimed.
  7. When to use this Zero one term and the agent becomes a reward maximiser.
